# The persistor

**A persistor is one cycle that creates the structure for the next cycle to build on.** It is the unit of Persistence Dynamics: the model of one unit of change. A cell, an immune response, a team and an organisation are each a persistor, and every persistor contains persistors and sits inside one.

In Arthur Koestler's word, a persistor is a **holon**: a whole to the parts below it and a part of the whole above it. Koestler gave every holon two tendencies, a self-assertive one that keeps it whole and an integrative one that joins it to something larger. In Persistence Dynamics these are the two operations, hold and cross, and the persistor adds what the holon left open: the order in which the two take turns, and the condition under which a round closes.

## One moment, four faces

A persistor is one moment in two dimensions, with four faces, and the moment repeats as a cycle. The transactional dimension and the generational dimension cross at one point. At the crossing, four things are true at once: the four regimes. They are not four events but four faces of one event, and the cycle is how that event repeats. Drawn as a square with the four faces at its corners, this is the persistor's geometric form: the quadragen.

## Five axioms

1. **Hold and cross.** A persistor has two operations at its boundary: hold, which keeps the self, and cross, which engages the other.
2. **Each active or latent.** Each operation is active or latent, so a persistor has two bits.
3. **Recursive.** Every persistor contains persistors and sits inside one. The same two bits appear at every level.
4. **Mass conserved.** Total mass across a closed system of persistors is constant.
5. **Motion exists.** Cycles run, gradients drive flows, and crossing engages.

φ is not an axiom. It appears when the cycle closes across levels.

## The formal core

Within one level a persistor is two coupled oscillations a quarter of a period apart. With θ the angular position on the cycle, starting at Potentiality,

<!-- eq: hold = (1 + cos <i>θ</i>) / 2, &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; cross = (1 + sin <i>θ</i>) / 2 -->

The trajectory is a circle. The four regimes are its quadrants, and the four thresholds are where it crosses an axis. Each regime spans a quarter of the circle and holds four positions, which gives sixteen positions in all. Within one level nothing is lost in a round; loss happens only across levels.

Across levels, closure fixes the ratio between one level and the next:

## Four thresholds

| Threshold | Bit that flips |
| --- | --- |
| Potentiality to Construction | hold goes latent |
| Construction to Encounter | cross goes latent |
| Encounter to Conservation | hold goes active |
| Conservation to Potentiality | cross goes active |

Each threshold is a discrete event: one bit flips and the next regime begins. At the last one the round closes, and what Conservation releases becomes the material the next Potentiality loads. **Without closure there is no persistor, and the closure is the seed of the next one.**

## How persistors nest

A closed persistor becomes one axis of the persistor one level up, and the axis keeps the direction of the cycle it came from. It takes two closed persistors to make the next one. The first to close is the **standing axis**; the second closes into it as the **arriving axis**. The partner decides which of the two diameters is carried up; the other is contained at its own level. While a persistor runs it is means for the one above; once it closes, its residue becomes part of that persistor's identity.
