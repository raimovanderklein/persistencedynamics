# The persistor

**Everything that persists is a persistor.** A cell, a flame, an immune response, a memory, a team, an institution: anything distinct enough to be given a name keeps its shape the same way. It runs one cycle that creates the structure for its next cycle to build on. A persistor is a thing together with that cycle: a cell is a persistor because it runs it, and stops being one when it stops. The persistor is the unit of Persistence Dynamics.

It is a large claim. It says that what lasts, at every scale, is built from one repeating unit, and that this unit is where space and time meet. The theory is set up so that the claim can be tested and could fail: [how it could be wrong](../theory/09-how-it-could-be-wrong.md).

## A whole and a part

Every persistor contains smaller persistors and sits inside a larger one. A T-cell runs inside an immune response, which runs inside a body, which runs inside a life. In Arthur Koestler's word, a persistor is a **holon**: a whole to the parts below it and a part of the whole above it. Koestler gave every holon two tendencies, a self-assertive one that keeps it whole and an integrative one that joins it to something larger. In Persistence Dynamics these are the two operations: **hold** keeps the self, and **cross** engages what is beyond it. What the persistor adds to the holon is the order in which the two take turns, and the condition under which a round closes.

## Where space and time meet

A persistor runs on two dimensions that cross at its centre. One spans space: taking in and giving out, between the system and what is beyond it. The other spans time: spending and carrying forward, between what the system was and what it will be. At any one instant a persistor has four faces around that crossing, one for each combination of hold and cross. Over time the four faces come in turn, as the four regimes of the cycle. Drawn as a square, with the four faces at its corners, this is the persistor's geometric form: the quadragen.

## How a persistor runs

| Regime | Hold | Cross | What happens | In an immune response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Potentiality | active | active | Material gathers against a threshold | A lymph node loads antigen |
| Construction | latent | active | One form is selected and built | One T-cell clone expands |
| Encounter | latent | latent | The built form meets what it was built for | T-cells engage the tumour |
| Conservation | active | latent | What worked is kept, what is done is released | Memory cells remain |

Between the regimes are four thresholds. At each one, exactly one of the two operations flips: hold goes latent, then cross, then hold comes back, then cross. At the last threshold the round closes, and what Conservation releases becomes the material the next Potentiality gathers. **Without closure there is no persistor, and the closure is the seed of the next one.**

## How persistors build on each other

When a persistor closes, it becomes part of the one above it. Seen from that level, the whole cycle is a single line, one axis of the larger cycle, and it takes two closed persistors to make the next one up. While a persistor runs, it is means: it works for the persistor that contains it. Once it closes, what it leaves behind becomes part of that persistor's identity. This is why a mature system differs from a young one. It is built from the residue of every cycle that ever closed inside it.

The theory works all of this out step by step, from the two operations to the scale between levels.
