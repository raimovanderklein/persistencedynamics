# The four regimes

| Regime | Hold | Cross | Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Potentiality | active | active | capacitor |
| Construction | latent | active | factory |
| Encounter | latent | latent | reactor |
| Conservation | active | latent | homeostat |

Each regime is a distinct kind of dynamical system, with its own observables, its own equation of motion and its own ways of failing. Plot hold against cross and one round of the cycle traces a circle. The four regimes are its four quadrants.

## Potentiality: the capacitor

Hold and cross are both active. Material gathers against a threshold. Below the threshold nothing happens; above it, everything that has gathered is released at once. Below the threshold the loading signal is a brake, and above it the same signal is the trigger. This is also how a flow becomes a clock: without a threshold, inflow and outflow would track each other and there would be no rhythm.

*Potentiality fails when the system will not release, or when it releases before enough has gathered.*

## Construction: the factory

Hold is latent and cross is active. The system has committed, and what remains is the work of building the form that Potentiality made possible. Candidates are selected, one is chosen and the form is made. Of the four regimes, only Construction turns a symmetric field of candidates into a ranking, so every sequence starts in Construction.

*Construction fails when selection never finishes, or when the chosen form does not fit the gap it was built for.*

## Encounter: the reactor

Hold and cross are both latent. The built form is in contact with what it was built to meet, at many sites in parallel, each with its own rate, yield and balance. Reach matters more than strength here: one site left uncovered can cancel the gains made at all the others.

*Encounter fails when the meeting does not arrive, when it arrives at the wrong target, or when it cannot end.*

## Conservation: the homeostat

Hold is active and cross is latent. What survived the Encounter is kept, and what is finished is released so that it does not burden the next round. The system holds a setpoint, watches for drift and corrects it, and the correction itself produces the perturbation that starts the next round. Conservation has two jobs at once: keep what worked, and let go of what is done.

*Conservation fails when nothing is released, or when what should have been kept is lost.*
