# The four regimes

| Regime | Hold | Cross | Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Potentiality | active | active | capacitor |
| Construction | latent | active | factory |
| Encounter | latent | latent | reactor |
| Conservation | active | latent | homeostat |

Each regime behaves like one familiar kind of system, its type, and each fails in its own ways. Plot hold against cross and one round of the cycle traces a circle. The four regimes are its quadrants.

## Potentiality: the capacitor

Hold and cross are both active. A capacitor stores charge until it discharges all at once. Material gathers against a threshold. Below the threshold the loading signal is a brake; above it, the same signal is the trigger and everything gathered is released in one event. This is how a steady flow becomes a rhythm: without a threshold, inflow and outflow would track each other and there would be no clock. *In an immune response: a lymph node loads antigen up to the threshold for priming T-cells.*

*Potentiality fails when it releases before enough has gathered, or never releases at all.*

## Construction: the factory

Hold is latent and cross is active. A factory turns input into a finished product. The system has committed to a direction; candidates are selected, one is chosen and its form is built. Only Construction turns a field of equal candidates into a ranking, so this is the regime where before and after, sequence itself, is made. *In an immune response: one T-cell clone is selected and expands.*

*Construction fails when selection never finishes, or when the chosen form does not fit what it must meet.*

## Encounter: the reactor

Hold and cross are both latent. That sounds like nothing is happening, but it is the opposite: the system holds nothing back and reaches for nothing further, because it is already in contact. A reactor runs many reactions side by side. The built form is in contact with what it was built for; the contact itself is the work, at many sites at once, each with its own rate and yield. Reach matters more than strength: one site left uncovered can cancel the gains at all the others. *In an immune response: T-cells engage the tumour.*

*Encounter fails when the meeting never arrives, arrives at the wrong target, or cannot end.*

## Conservation: the homeostat

Hold is active and cross is latent. A homeostat holds a setpoint by watching for drift and correcting it. What survived the Encounter is kept, and what is finished is released. The correction itself produces the disturbance that starts the next round. *In an immune response: the tumour is cleared, and a memory population is kept for next time.*

*Conservation fails when nothing is released, or when what should have been kept is lost.*
