## The four regimes

| Regime | Hold | Cross | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potentiality | active | active | Capacitor |
| Construction | latent | active | Factory |
| Encounter | latent | latent | Reactor |
| Conservation | active | latent | Homeostat |

Each regime is a distinct kind of dynamical system. The type names on the right are the operational shorthand the framework uses to talk about what each regime *does*: a capacitor loads and discharges, a factory selects and assembles, a reactor runs parallel reactions, a homeostat maintains a setpoint while detecting drift. The four types are the four basic dynamical systems that fall out of the two-bit state-space, and each has its own observables, its own equations, and its own characteristic failure modes.

> **Structure — *Each regime has a distinct temporal signature.***
>
> Reading each regime as a (past-loading, future-loading) pair on the state vector:
>
> – **Potentiality**: past = high, future = high. Both projections fully loaded, no release yet.
> – **Construction**: past = released, future = high. The accumulated state has been spent; forward gradient is doing all the work.
> – **Encounter**: past = released, future = released. Both projections at zero; the system is at the meeting point where contact happens.
> – **Conservation**: past = rebuilding, future = released. The state vector is reaccumulating from the encounter's residue; forward gradient is dormant.
>
> One round of the cycle is one complete revolution through the (past, future) phase space. The four regimes are the four quadrants of that space.

Potentiality is the loaded state in which both operations are at peak. Construction is the regime in which hold has surrendered and cross is doing all the work. Encounter is the moment when both operations go quiet, because what was built has met what it was built for. Conservation is the inverse of Construction: hold is doing the work, cross has gone latent, and what survived the encounter is being kept while the substrate for the next round is prepared.

The four are not stages of writing a draft. They are four different things a system can be doing, and a persisting system has to do all four to close one round. None can be skipped without the cycle breaking, and none is more important than the others, since each produces exactly the material the next one needs.

### Potentiality — the capacitor

[ANIMATION: a chamber filling to threshold, then releasing in a single discrete event]

The system is fully loaded. Material has been gathered against a threshold, the threshold has not yet been crossed, and the weight of what has accumulated is pulling toward release. Below the threshold nothing happens; above it, everything that has built up releases at once. The dynamical signature of a capacitor is this sign-flip at threshold: below it, the loading signal is a brake; above it, the same signal is the trigger. Examples that sit comfortably in this regime: a lymph node loading antigen, a manuscript unwritten but already shaped in the writer's head, a team that has gathered all the information and is one conversation away from a decision.

A capacitor is also what makes a flow into a clock. Without a threshold, inflow and outflow would track each other continuously and produce no rhythm. The threshold breaks steady flow into discrete cycles. Every clock the framework has examined, biological or otherwise, has a capacitor somewhere at its origin.

*Fails when the system will not release, or when it releases before enough has gathered.*

### Construction — the factory

[ANIMATION: parallel lines selecting candidates, assembling, and finishing the chosen form]

Hold has let go. The system has committed, and what is left is the work of building the form that the previous regime made possible. Selection runs; one candidate is chosen; the form gets made. The dynamical signature of a factory is steady-flow with parallel tracks and a selection gate: throughput on the lines, differentiation across tracks, yield at the gate. An immune clone expands. A draft becomes a document. A protocol gets specified in enough detail that someone could actually run it. What comes out of Construction is finished, licensed, and ready to be put to use.

Construction is also the regime where order is created. Of the four regimes, only Construction takes a symmetric field of candidates and produces a ranking. Every before-and-after the framework recognises traces back to a Construction event somewhere; sequence does not pre-exist its own manufacture.

*Fails when selection never finishes, or when the chosen form does not fit what is needed.*

### Encounter — the reactor

[ANIMATION: the built form meeting what it was made for, at many points at once]

Both operations have gone quiet because the meeting is happening. The built form is in contact with what it was built to meet, whether the tumour, the market, the reader, or the disease, and the question is whether the contact does what it was supposed to do. The dynamical signature of a reactor is parallel reaction sites: many small reactions running concurrently, each with its own rate, yield, and balance. Reach matters here more than strength. A single sub-position where coverage is missing cancels gains made everywhere else. This is the regime where most clinical trials and most product launches succeed or fail, and it is the regime that the framework spent the longest learning to read accurately.

*Fails when the meeting does not arrive, when it arrives at the wrong target, or when it cannot end.*

### Conservation — the homeostat

[ANIMATION: keeping what survived, releasing what is finished, and feeding the next round]

Hold is doing the work again, but in a different mode than in Potentiality. What survived the encounter is being kept, whether that is the memory cell, the institutional method, the habit, or the lesson learned, and what is finished is being released, so that it does not contaminate the next round. The dynamical signature of a homeostat is multi-layer maintenance with drift-detection feedback: the system holds a setpoint, watches for departure, applies correction, and the correcting activity itself produces the perturbation that initiates the next cycle. Conservation has two jobs at once, and the dual responsibility is what makes it the hardest regime to read in any actual system. Hold what worked. Let go of what is done. Both, simultaneously.

*Fails when nothing is released, or when what should have been kept gets dropped.*
