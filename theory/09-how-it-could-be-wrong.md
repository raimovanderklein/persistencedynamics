# How it could be wrong

The closure equation depends on no material, so the theory can be tested in any system it claims to describe. A persisting system whose geometry differs from this account disproves it. These are the predictions that measurement can break.

| What to measure | What the theory predicts | What would disprove it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The ratio between two nested levels | *d* = 1/φ ≈ 0.618 | Levels that contract by a different ratio, consistently |
| Carried and contained across a level | carried² + contained² = *R*² | The sum not holding across several levels of the same cycle |
| Transitions between regimes | Exactly one operation flips per step | A transition that flips both at once |
| Damping per cycle, γ*T* (damping rate γ times period *T*), for four nested levels | 4 · ln φ ≈ 1.924 | A persistent difference under controlled conditions |

Real cycles deviate from these numbers, and the deviation is where the particular substrate shows itself. The structural values are the reference to read deviations against, not a claim that every system returns them exactly.
