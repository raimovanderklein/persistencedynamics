# The two operations

A persisting system does two things at its boundary. It **holds**: it gathers, contains and keeps what it has. It **crosses**: it reaches out, releases and hands forward. These are the only two moves a system can make if it is both to keep something and to pass something on. A breath does both. A cell does both at its membrane. A team does both when it keeps an option open and then commits.

Each operation is, at any moment, either **active** or **latent**. Active means it is doing work now. Latent means it is present in the system but not doing work now. Two operations with two states each give four configurations, and each configuration is a regime.

Run in time, hold and cross are two oscillations of the same period, one quarter of a period apart. When one is at its peak, the other is passing its midpoint. Each operation is active while its wave is above the midpoint and latent while it is below.

## Hold reads as past, cross reads as future

Hold is what the system has accumulated: its identity, its lineage, its memory, the form it has already built. It acts on the time-reversed part of the system's state. Cross is what the system reaches toward: the meeting that has not yet happened. It acts on the time-forward part, the gradient toward the next configuration. Read this way, Potentiality holds the most past while reaching for the most future. Encounter releases both and is pure present. Conservation rebuilds the past while cross stays latent.
