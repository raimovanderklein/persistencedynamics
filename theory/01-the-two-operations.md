# The two operations

A persistor does two things. It **holds**: it keeps what it has gathered present, configured and ready to use. A membrane holds the inside of a cell; an immune system holds the memory of past infections. It **crosses**: it reaches across its own boundary to engage something beyond it. A cell signals to its neighbours; a T-cell engages its target. Holding keeps the system continuous with its own past. Crossing makes it continuous with what comes next. Both cost work, and a system that stops doing either stops persisting.

At any moment each operation is **active**, doing work now, or **latent**, set up but not doing work now. Latent is not absent: a latent hold can be switched back on. Two operations with two states each give four combinations, and each combination is a regime.

Over one round of the cycle, hold and cross behave like two waves of the same period, one quarter of a period apart. When one is at its peak, the other is passing its midpoint.
